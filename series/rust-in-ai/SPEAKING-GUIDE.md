# How to talk so it sounds human

Reference (Nov 2026): George Alexander, "If nobody watches your videos, do this." (5:17, 1.1 M views). Full analysis: `planning/research/2026-11-05-reference-george-alexander.md`.
This replaces the old Better Stack guide (short 8-16 word fact lines, "never say basically"), which made our scripts sound choppy.

## What he does (measured from his transcript)
- **Speed:** about 200 wpm after jump cuts, but it never feels rushed, because sentences flow into each other. **Our target stays 150 to 165 wpm**; copy his sentence shape, not his speed.
- **Sentence length:** average about 20 words, mixed widely (4 to 38). A long, flowing sentence, then a short one to land it ("And I love doing that.").
- **Almost every sentence starts with a link word:** So, And, But then, Because, Whereas, And suddenly, Okay so, Well. The listener always knows how the new sentence relates to the last one.
- **He says the viewer's thoughts out loud:** "Maybe YouTube's too saturated. Maybe the algorithm just hates me. Maybe I'm not talented enough."
- **He talks from his own experience first, then gives the rule:** "I'll move the camera around the room 20 times trying to get the right shot." Concrete, with a number, slightly funny.
- **Small spoken softeners:** "I think", "you know", "like", "basically", "almost", "probably", "really simple". About one every 3 or 4 sentences. They mark opinion as opinion and sound like a person thinking.
- **Natural repetition:** "I've definitely, definitely been through periods like that myself." Not cleaned up.
- **Two people, same situation, different thinking**, with their thoughts in quotes, then a question to the viewer: "Same number of videos, same number of views, but which creator do you think is more likely to grow?"
- **One memorable line per part:** "Create like an artist, but study like a scientist." It is then shown on screen.
- **Callbacks:** "numbers on a screen" comes back three times. It ties the video together.
- **Each part ends with a question that opens the next one:** "But what happens when it's not just one video?" / "Okay, so now there is one final question that we need to answer."
- **The ending is one small physical action:** "take a piece of paper and literally write down one question."
- **Warm close, no begging:** "Okay, so hopefully you enjoyed this video. I had a lot of fun making it." Then one video to watch next.

## Delivery
- **Talk to one person**, a friend who asked you about this news, not "guys" or an audience.
- **Pace:** 150 to 165 wpm. Speed up a little on the story, slow down on a number or a name.
- **Breath:** one thought per breath. A long sentence is fine if it is one thought with "and" or "because" in it.
- **Pitch:** fall at the end of a statement. Rise only on a real question.
- **Stress one word per sentence** (the number, the name, or the surprise). Captions highlight the same word.
- **Allowed and good:** "So", "You know", "I think", "Honestly", "Okay, so", "basically" (once or twice a video). Not allowed: "Hey guys", "Welcome back", "smash that like button", "without further ado".
- **Pauses:** leave a short pause before each new part and after the memorable line. The edit keeps those; it cuts the rest.
- **Face:** let the face react. Smile on the warm lines, a small frown on the bad news, hands open when you explain.
- **Eyes:** lens only. Teleprompter right under the lens.

## Practice routine (15 min before recording)
1. Read the hook five times, record each, keep the one that sounds most like you talking to a friend.
2. Read each [CAM] line once looking away from the script: say the idea in your own words, then read it. If your own words were better, tell Claude, and the script changes.
3. Mark the stress word in each sentence.
4. Record in sections, retakes welcome (last take wins).
5. Re-record the hook last, when you are warm.
